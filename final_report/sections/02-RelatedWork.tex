\section{Related Work}

Emotion classification from facial expressions is a well-established problem in computer vision and has been extensively studied over the past decades. This section reviews relevant work in facial emotion recognition, deep learning approaches for face analysis, and recent advancements in transformer-based architectures.

\subsection{Facial Emotion Recognition}

Early approaches to facial emotion recognition relied on hand-crafted features and classical machine learning methods. Traditional methods used features such as Local Binary Patterns (LBP), Histogram of Oriented Gradients (HOG), and geometric feature extraction, combined with classifiers like Support Vector Machines (SVM) or Random Forests.

With the advancement of deep learning, Convolutional Neural Networks (CNNs) have become the dominant approach for facial expression recognition. Pre-trained models on large-scale datasets like ImageNet have shown strong performance when fine-tuned for emotion classification tasks.

\subsection{Deep Learning for Facial Analysis}

Recent work has demonstrated the effectiveness of deep neural networks for facial analysis tasks. ResNet, VGG, and Inception architectures have been widely adopted and adapted for emotion recognition. Attention mechanisms and squeeze-and-excitation blocks have further improved model performance by allowing networks to focus on informative facial regions.

\subsection{Transformer Architectures}

Vision Transformers (ViT) and Compact Transformers represent recent advancements in visual representation learning. These architectures have shown promising results across various computer vision tasks, including facial analysis. Compact Transformers offer computational efficiency compared to standard transformers while maintaining competitive performance.

\subsection{Datasets and Benchmarks}

Several publicly available datasets have been instrumental in advancing facial emotion recognition research, including:
\begin{itemize}
    \item CK+ (Extended Cohn-Kanade Database)
    \item FERPlus (Facial Expression Recognition in the wild)
    \item AffectNet
    \item RAF-DB (Real-world Affective Faces Database)
    \item ExpW (Facial Expression in-the-wild)
\end{itemize}

These datasets vary in terms of image quality, diversity of subjects, and annotation protocols, providing different challenges for model development and evaluation.
